# AussieTools — first tradie feedback round

Goal: find whether a tradie can enter a real job, understand the result and make a useful quoting decision without help.

## Who to test with

Start with 5 tradies: a sole operator, an employer/apprentice supervisor, someone who quotes fixed-price work, someone who charges hourly and someone with significant travel. Use phones for at least 3 sessions. Ask them to use a completed job or anonymised realistic figures. Do not collect customer names or financial records.

## Ten-minute session

1. Send the calculator link. Ask: “Imagine you are about to quote a job. Can you work out whether it pays enough?” Do not explain the inputs first.
2. Have them enter their own example. Observe where they pause, ask questions or miss a cost.
3. Ask them to explain job profit, profit margin and profit per owner hour in their own words.
4. Ask them to include GST, adjust their margin goal and try a job taking longer.
5. Ask: “Would this change your quote? What is missing before you would trust it?”

## Questions to capture

- Was your hourly cost clear, or did you enter your usual charge-out rate?
- Did you understand that your own labour is already deducted before profit?
- Were recoverable GST and costs excluding GST clear?
- How would you allocate overhead to this job?
- Does profit per owner hour help, or does it sound like your wage?
- Are travel time, subcontractors, equipment hire, contingency or callbacks missing?
- Would you use it on your next quote? Why?

## Prioritise changes

Fix any wrong calculation first. Next fix repeated misunderstandings that change a quote. Then address completion barriers and missing costs raised by multiple participants. Keep cosmetic preferences separate. Do not add more tools during this round.

## Ready for the next milestone

At least 4 of 5 participants complete the flow without explanation, understand that owner labour has been deducted, and can explain the margin/quote recommendation. Recheck affected calculations and mobile flow after changes. Treat five sessions as an initial usability signal, not proof of market demand.
